Noah Solomon

Hi! I’m Noah Solomon, a recent graduate (class of 2026) with a Bachelor’s in Computer Science and Electrical & Computer Engineering from Worcester Polytechnic Institute. I am based in Seattle and seeking employment, preferably here.

I’m interested in many aspects of both CS and ECE, but I would say I am most drawn to the mechanics of computing: on the CS side, programming language theory/design and compilers, and, on the ECE side, FPGAs and hardware security. I am also passionate about making tools that are accessible to people with varying levels of computer skills.

Projects

This past year I completed my senior project, developing more intuitive and efficient software for the Vernam Lab’s photon emission microscope for use in sidechannel analysis/hardware security research. I worked closely throughout the year with another student team, who used my in-development software for a successful proof-of-concept attack on an FPGA hardware implementation of an LLM, showing that the LLM’s weights are vulnerable to theft. Our joint video about the project won first place in the ECE department’s video competition.

Also this past year, I independently developed an interactive FPGA board simulator fully compatible with Mac, Windows, and Linux, which runs users’ Verilog code on-screen on a “virtual devkit.” This is more engaging for students experience than purely teaching testbenches, without needing to buy expensive FPGAs. On course staff for ECE 2029 (Introduction to Digital Circuit Design), I helped students install the software, which was used for all of their lab assignments. Multiple professors are interested in using the software again, and I am continuing its development in my free time.
